Domain-tuned models rather than a general chatbot: financial sentiment, technical pattern discovery, operational anomaly detection, geospatial signal extraction.

03

A market twin, an operations twin, an asset twin. Each venture renders its domain as something you can look at and move through before committing.

04

01 — Who we are

An operator, not a fund

We do not write cheques and wait. Every venture in the portfolio is founded, staffed, built and shipped by the same three people — which means the constraint on us is not capital, it is engineering time.

That constraint is the whole reason the company is shaped the way it is. A kernel is the part of a system everything else runs on, and we build that part first: pipelines that ingest hostile real-world data, models tuned to one domain rather than all of them, simulations a person can actually look at, and a decision layer that hands back an answer with its reasoning attached. Four moves. Every venture repeats them.

From the outside, a trading terminal, an SME control tower and a Gulf property dossier have nothing to do with each other. Underneath, they are the same machine running five times over. That is not a marketing framing — it is why the portfolio is possible at all.

A kernel is the part of a system everything else runs on. We build that part first.

What that means in practice

Three ventures — AIVEX Analytics, Aivex Chart and Aivex News — share one engineering lineage. One of them, Aivex News, literally runs as both a standalone service and a module inside another product, from the same codebase, with no source changes.

That is the test we hold ourselves to: if a component cannot be lifted out and dropped back in, it was not built as a kernel.

02 — What we believe

Five rules we actually follow

These are not values on a wall. Each one is visible in the code of at least one shipped product, and each one has cost us something to keep.

01

Build the core once

Aivex News is the proof: one codebase with two deployment modes — its own database and entry points, or dropped into the AIVEX core where it re-binds to shared database, config, logging and metrics without a single source change. If it needs a fork, it was not a kernel.

02

Integrate, don't replace

FlowOps sits beside Logo, Mikro and Netsis rather than asking an SME to migrate. Athar reads the registries brokers already work from. Aivex Chart wraps its engine without editing one file inside it. Adoption cost is a product decision here, not a sales problem.

03

Clean the data first, or refuse

Dirty data is not a downstream inconvenience, it is the first-layer problem. FlowOps locks its predictions when the data quality score drops below threshold rather than producing a confident wrong answer. A model that cannot be trusted should say so.

04

Explain the output

A signal shows what moved it and which gate let it through. A control tower shows why an order turned red. A property score links every claim back to the record it came from. Where we cannot justify a number, we say the direction is known and the magnitude is not.

05

Ship the honest version

Aivex Chart labels every result a Real Core Run or a Demo Fixture Run and never presents a fixture as live market truth. Our limitations are printed on the product pages, not hidden behind a demo. It is a slower way to sell and the only way we are willing to.

06

Degrade gracefully

AIVEX runs without market-data API keys. Aivex Chart runs with zero credentials on deterministic fixtures. Aivex News keeps going when a source fails and records the failure. Nothing in the portfolio needs a perfect environment to be useful.

01 — System overview

Pipeline-driven, not service-mesh-driven

AIVEX is composed of independent Python services and a Next.js frontend. Each module owns its domain and communicates through a shared MySQL database — modules read each other's tables by convention. There is no message bus, and that is a deliberate choice: one database, one source of truth, no delivery semantics to reason about.

Data flows top to bottom. Ingestion writes raw tables; metrics computes features from them; the signal engine composes signals; the governor gates them and writes gating.json, which the watchdog reads to restart or disable processes.

03 — Data flow

From a wire story to a gated signal

  1. Ingestion

    Runs on schedule via Main.py or the watchdog. news/ writes articles and AI sentiment scores to haberler; chart/ writes price bars and indicators to ohlcv_bars and ohlcv_latest; companies/ writes fundamentals to sirketler and financials_normalized.

  2. Feature computation

    metrics/ reads the ingested tables and computes 31 features — volatility, momentum, liquidity, regime and market breadth — into features_latest, raw_metric_points and symbol_daily_features.

  3. Signal composition

    signal_engine/ reads features plus raw data and writes per-module atomic signals to atomic_signals_log, then composes the high-conviction survivors into final_signals.

  4. Risk management

    governor/ reads final_signals and evaluates PnL, drawdown and drift, then writes governor/outputs/gating.json — a kill switch plus module-disable instructions. The watchdog reads that file and restarts or stops processes accordingly.

  5. Monitoring

    eye/ reads every module's tables and serves the developer dashboard on port 8001. brain/ reads chart and metrics data to propose pattern candidates — which require human approval before they ever reach the signal engine.

01 — What it is

A product layer over an engine we never touch

The Chart Core stays the analytical brain. Aivex Chart adds authentication, a product API, cycle orchestration, job tracking, a chart terminal, a historical lab and a strategy builder — all through one adapter. No file inside aivex_analytics/ or aivex-eye-service/ is modified.

Everything above the dashed line is the product. Everything below it is the engine, imported and never edited. The adapter is the only place the two meet — which is what makes REAL and DEMO interchangeable.

PRINCIPLE 01

The engine stays untouched

When the sibling aivex_analytics repository is present, the adapter imports the real TechnicalEngine (verified at engine v2.0.0) and labels every result a Real Core Run. When it is absent, deterministic fixtures take over and results are labelled Demo Fixture Run. The UI never presents a demo finding as live market truth.

PRINCIPLE 02

The demo works offline

Deterministic OHLCV fixtures for AAPL, GLD, SLV and USO plus product-layer fallbacks mean the whole flow runs with no MySQL, no API keys and no external services. Live market data via yfinance is opt-in and off by default.

04 — Honest limits

What V0 does not do yet

Stating these up front is part of the product. Every limitation below is visible in the UI rather than hidden behind a demo.

Known limitations

V0, August 2026.

  • The Historical Lab uses a deterministic product-layer research fallback, not the core Model Horizon Lab — which needs a populated MySQL dataset we cannot provision read-only. Clearly labelled DEMO.
  • Live market data (yfinance) is opt-in and off by default; the demo uses fixtures and synthesis.
  • Auth is a local demo account — PBKDF2 with opaque tokens. /forgot-password email delivery is stubbed.
  • The continuous scheduler runs in-process on a single node.
  • V0 surfaces AAPL only, though the domain model and universe config are ready for GLD, SLV and USO.

Next milestones

In order of expected value.

  1. Wire the core Model Horizon Lab through the adapter once a dataset path exists.

  2. Surface the multi-instrument universe and concurrent universe runs. Cycles already accept a universe, so this is a UI change, not a re-architecture.

  3. Persisted, authenticated multi-user accounts with JWT refresh.

  4. WebSocket push for run progress, replacing polling.

  5. An optional MySQL adapter to read the core's real ohlcv_bars and indicator_snapshots.

01 — The pipeline

Two speeds of intelligence in one pass

Every run does six things to every article. A failure in the accumulator, the stream or the context build is logged but never aborts the run or loses ingested articles.

StageWhat happens
IngestPulls headlines from all sources in parallel, deduplicates by URL and against the database, then extracts full article content with multiple fallback strategies.
AnalyzeScores sentiment (Positive / Negative / Neutral) with FinBERT, extracts ticker symbols, matches articles to tracked companies, and tags themes — crypto, macro, geopolitical and so on.
AccumulateMerges batch statistics into running daily totals and emits hot signals to Redis the moment sentiment spikes.
StreamPublishes enriched articles to the Redis stream news:articles for downstream consumers.
PersistSaves everything to MySQL with company links and extraction metadata.
Roll upBuilds a daily world-context snapshot and compresses history into weekly and monthly narratives, LLM-assisted.
FAST PATH

Accumulator — every batch, no LLM

Real-time anomaly detection and hot signals. Cheap enough to run on every single batch, which is what makes the signal timely rather than retrospective.

SLOW PATH

World context — daily, weekly, monthly

Narrative generation and regime-shift analysis. LLM-assisted, run on a schedule, and never in the way of ingestion.

Non-English articles — Turkish sources in particular — are auto-detected and translated to English before scoring, so a financial-English model still works on them.

02 — Embedding

One codebase, two lives

Aivex News is the standalone build of the AIVEX news module, and it is designed to drop straight back into the AIVEX core at core/services/news/. It never hard-imports AIVEX: every coupling is a try/except that resolves to AIVEX when present and to a standalone fallback otherwise.

The database is injected into run(db, ...) — the pipeline body never names a concrete database class. Point NEWSINTEL_DB_FACTORY at any module.path:callable that implements the nine-method NewsDB surface and the pipeline runs against it unchanged.

01 — The problem

Garbage in, garbage out — and nobody in the building to fix it

More than 80% of SMEs in Türkiye and worldwide already run an ERP. In practice those systems act as cumbersome digital archives of historical records rather than decision-support mechanisms. The enterprise AI products gaining ground — SAP Joule and its peers — miss this market entirely: high consulting costs, long integrations, and an exclusive focus on enterprise-scale companies.

BOTTLENECK 01

Dirty data

Data entry in SMEs is mostly manual. Delivery dates are left blank; warehouse counts are logged late. It is impossible to run AI analytics on uncleaned data — even the world's best model produces garbage results from it.

BOTTLENECK 02

High barriers

Replacing an existing ERP with a smart global system means millions of lira in licensing, months of implementation, and the risk of operational downtime. For an SME that is not a trade-off, it is a non-starter.

BOTTLENECK 03

No BI headcount

A company with 20–200 employees has neither the budget nor the focus to hire a dedicated business intelligence specialist, data scientist or data engineer.

02 — Architecture

One pipeline, three layers, each feeding the next

A single holistic architecture where every layer sequentially feeds the one above it. Actions and approvals executed in the Decide layer feed back into Detect, reinforcing the anomaly models and improving the data quality score autonomously over time.

01 · DETECT

Data Quality Engine

The foundation. It runs the moment it connects to the ERP: unfilled delivery dates from the last three months, inventory records that do not match physical counts, invoice anomalies.

By telling the user that AI forecasting cannot run until these records are corrected, it enforces operational discipline. To reduce the friction that causes the problem in the first place, it offers smart auto-fill suggestions — predicting a missing delivery date from past supplier performance, for instance.

02 · SEE

Visual Logistics Control Tower

Maps the cleaned data onto a single radar-style screen. Orders and warehouses carry a traffic-light state: green for problem-free, yellow for risky, red for delayed.

This is the layer that makes the system legible to someone who has never opened a BI tool — and the one that carries a demo.

03 · DECIDE

AI Decision Copilot

Frees managers from navigating menus. Ask “which product group carries an inventory risk in the Ankara warehouse next week?” and the system does not just answer.

It recommends: “Raw material group X is at 82% risk. Consider an urgent order with supplier Y, whose delivery rate is 15% faster.”

03 — Eight layers

What gets collected, simultaneously

①

Identity & title

Parcel and building number, area, ownership type (freehold, leasehold, musataha), title history, mortgage and lien annotations where available, building age, floor and unit breakdown, developer.

②

Price & comparables

Transacted prices from official title records — not listings. Radius and similarity-based comp selection, an AVM estimate with a confidence interval and the weighting rationale, rental yield, listing-to-transaction gap, 12/36/60-month trend, liquidity and volume.

③

News & events

The main differentiator. Geo-tagged, impact-scored news from local EN/AR press, government wires (WAM, SPA, QNA), municipal decisions, exchange filings and tender portals — each mapped to a location, a distance, an impact direction and a magnitude estimate.

④

Infrastructure & masterplan

Metro and tram lines (existing, planned, under construction), roads, airports, schools, hospitals, malls, mega-projects (NEOM, Diriyah, Qiddiya, Al Maktoum, Lusail), municipal masterplan layers, zoning, FAR and height limits.

⑤

Natural resources & environment

Coastline distance and 3D sea-view analysis, groundwater depth and salinity, soil type and sabkha zones — which materially move foundation cost in the Gulf — green space access, oil and gas fields, industrial proximity, sun, shade, prevailing wind and dust corridors.

⑥

Risk

Flood maps (critical since the January 2024 UAE floods), elevation and drainage, sea-level projection, seismicity (notably Oman and Musandam), summer urban heat island, noise from airport and motorway corridors, and construction disturbance nearby.

⑦

Socioeconomics & demand

Population and density, nationality mix, income proxies, workplace density, POI diversity and walkability, school quality, short-term rental occupancy and average daily rate.

⑧

Regulation & ownership

Freehold zone status, foreign ownership rules, golden visa thresholds, transfer fees (DLD 4%, KSA RETT 5%), new escrow and off-plan rules, the rental index (RERA / Ejar), and short-term-rental licence status.

The four hard problems

Each of these is a build risk, not a detail.

  • Parcel resolution. Every country has its own address scheme — Makani in Dubai, Onwani/Wasel in Abu Dhabi, the Saudi national address, PACI in Kuwait. Solved with a per-country resolver adapter behind one canonical PropertyID.
  • Arabic/English bilinguality. Place-name transliteration is a nightmare — “Al Barsha”, “AlBarsha”, “البرشاء”. Solved with transliteration normalisation, a geo-alias dictionary and embedding-based matching.
  • Comp sparsity. A small district may see two transactions in 90 days. Solved with hierarchical fallback — building, then community, then district, then a similar-profile district — and by showing the uncertainty rather than hiding it.
  • News impact inference. An impact score is speculative and invites hallucination. The LLM is used for classification and entity extraction only; magnitude comes from historical event studies, and where the data is thin the product says “direction known, magnitude uncertain”.
05 — Roadmap & model

From one city to the region

  1. Phase 0 — Discovery · 4–6 weeks

    A data access audit per country (licence plus a real API test), 15–20 broker interviews, a clickable prototype, and a competitor price and coverage matrix.

  2. Phase 1 — MVP · 3–4 months · Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh

    Parcel and Makani search plus map selection; layers ①, ② with a simple AVM, ③ geo-tagged news, ④ infrastructure proximity; agency-branded dossier PDF. Target: 25 paying agencies.

  3. Phase 2 — Depth · +3 months

    Layers ⑤ and ⑥, portfolio watchlists and alerts, AVM calibration with backtesting against a MAPE target under 8%, and Qatar plus Bahrain.

  4. Phase 3 — Scale · +4 months

    Oman, Kuwait and the northern emirates; a public API and white-label widget; bank and valuation-firm integration; a full Arabic interface.

  5. Phase 4 — Platform

    A developer feasibility module, tokenisation and fractional-ownership integration through REGA's RER, and CRM integrations — Salesforce, PropSpace, Bitrix.
